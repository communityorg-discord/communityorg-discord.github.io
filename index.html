How we work

We live in different countries in the UK and we still run the same things. Dion is in England. Evan is in Wales. The work is remote, on Discord, and on the systems we built so a community can be run properly instead of from somebody’s memory.

We do not split “one of us owns it, the other helps.” Both of us founded Community Organisation. Both of us own FSIT. The split is what each of us actually does with our hands.

Community Organisation

Community Organisation is what we started. It is a Discord community with real structure behind it: departments, standards, staff, and tools built in-house rather than a pile of public bots holding the place together. The idea is simple. A community with room for the next person, run like it matters.

That is the through-line of our past. We did not just sit in a server. We built the organisation around it: how people join, how staff are recorded, how mail and sign-in work, how leadership is actually organised. Ownership sits with us. The day-to-day of the organisation is departments, not a group chat with extra steps.

The best-known community out of that is United States Government Roleplay (USGRP). It is a serious, story-driven US government roleplay Discord: departments, courts, law enforcement, an economy, and a chain of command people actually have to follow. Dion is President in that RP. Evan is White House Chief of Staff. The rest of the country is staffed by the community.

CO is being rebuilt: new look, same idea. The public site is communityorg.co.uk.

Fortnite Sprite Info & Trading

FSIT is ours as well, and it is a separate thing. It is a Discord for Fortnite sprites: collecting, indexing, trading, and a staff team that keeps it usable. It is not Community Organisation, and it is not USGRP. Same two people, different community, different bots.

Spritely is the sprite bot: catalogue, who has what, the leaderboard, trading, and Sprite Operations requests. Utilities is the server bot: tickets, staff records, moderation, reports, and protection. Both are fan projects. They are not made by, endorsed by, or connected to Epic Games.

Spritely’s public page is sprites.codes.
