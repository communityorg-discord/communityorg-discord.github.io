Evan: That's the chart. Hierarchy stayed out of the video so I can walk it with you — ask now, don't guess later. Six things: the six teams, every position, who your supervisor is, what each team can do, how you move up, and what happens when you disagree. Six teams: Senior Management, Administration, Moderation, Support, Community Engagement, and Sprite Operations. Everyone has one main team. Some of you hold a second position — I'll come back to that. Senior Management is the top: Owners, Director of Operations, and Executive Assistant to the Director. Those three are handed out by the owners only — no command, and nobody below them can appoint into them. Administration is four deep: Head Administrator, Senior Administrator, Administrator, Junior Administrator. Head Admin reports to the Executive Assistant, then Director, then Owners. From Junior Admin to the top: Junior Admin, Admin, Senior Admin, Head Admin, Executive Assistant, Director, Owner. One step at a time. Moderation sits under Administration. Three positions: Moderator, Senior Moderator, Head Moderator. Moderator goes to Senior, Senior goes to Head, Head reports into Administration at Junior Administrator. Support sits alongside Moderation the same way: Support Officer, Senior Support Officer, Head Support Officer. Same chain — Head reports into Administration at Junior Administrator. Separate teams: a Support Officer does not answer to a Moderator, and the other way round. Side by side, not above each other. Community Engagement: Officer, Senior Officer, Manager. Officer to Senior, Senior to Manager, Manager to the Executive Assistant. That team sits above Administration on the chart, not through it. Sprite Operations is run by the Head — owners only hand that out. Under the Head: Lead Distributor, Lead Mastery, Lead Hunter, each with their own team. If you're a Distributor your supervisor is the Lead Distributor, not the Head. Leads go to the Head; the Head reports up from there. Second positions: some of you hold two — a Moderator who is also a Distributor, for example. You're on both teams, so you have two supervisors. Take the question to whichever one it's about. Don't take a sprite question to a Senior Moderator just because you talk to them more.

Evan: A position is not a permission. Moderation can warn, mute and unmute. Administration can do all of that, plus kick, ban, temporary ban, and viewing and editing cases. That changed tonight — an Admin used to be able to ban but not warn. Anything a Moderator can do, an Admin can do. Senior Management can do all of it, and is the only team that can remove a case entirely. Support and Community Engagement have no punishment permissions — that's deliberate. Ticket permissions are separate: given to people, not teams. Support Officers handle tickets, not bans. If it needs a punishment, escalate it. Not sure what you hold? Run /perm — it shows your permissions and where each one comes from. Don't guess from your role colour. A Discord role is not a permission. The bot checks a list.

Evan: This is the chart: every team, every position, a line up to who it reports to. Your supervisor is the box directly above yours — that's the whole rule. Nicknames are on, not real names. Each box has a PNI — Position Number Identifier. The number is the level, same row same level. The letter is the seat: the three Owner seats are PNI001-A, B and C. No letter means there's only one of that position. Quote the PNI when you mean a seat. An empty box is an open seat, not a mistake. If the box above you is empty, your supervisor is the next filled box up the line. Quick recap so you hear your own. Moderators — Senior Moderator. Senior Moderators — Head Moderator. Support Officers — Senior Support. Senior Support — Head Support. Both Heads — Junior Administrator. Community Engagement — Senior, then Manager. Distributors, Mastery, Hunters — your lead, then the Head. Admins — one rung up each time. If I missed yours, ask now. The chart is live off the staff records — not a picture, and it doesn't get updated by hand. If it's wrong, the record is wrong. Open a Senior Management ticket and we'll fix it. After the meeting this chart goes out to all staff. Check where you sit and who your supervisor is any time.

Evan: Promotions and demotions go through the bot. You can only move someone below your rank, and only hand out a position below your own. No promoting yourself, no promoting a peer. Owner, Director, Executive Assistant, Head Admin, Senior Admin, Admin and Head of Sprite Operations are superusers only. Same on punishments: the bot won't let you warn, mute, kick or ban someone at your rank or above. Superusers are the only exception. If a command refuses you on someone senior, escalate it. The hierarchy is there to be followed. If your lead gives an answer, that's the answer. Don't go around them to me or Dion, and don't go over our heads if you don't like ours. Disagree privately, once, with the person who decided. That's the chain. Tickets: support, staff applications, sprite indexing — all through the panels. Claim what you pick up so two people aren't on the same one. You cannot claim or close your own. If it's above you, /escalate it. Moderation is through the commands: /warn, /kick, /mute, /ban. Each one creates a numbered case. /case to view it, change the reason, or remove it. No manual actions. If it isn't logged, it didn't happen. /promote and /demote change the roles — the chart redraws itself off the records. Protection — automod, anti-nuke, alt detection — runs in the background. None of it bans on its own. It flags, we decide. That's Utilities. Dion will explain Spritely.
